# ReflowEconomy STEM check

Verdict: the reference micro-factory (option A) sits within known physics, chemistry, biology and mathematics on its mass balance, float-sink separation, press heating, fume capture, duct and fan, guarding, structure and economics, and nothing fails outright, but the check is **open**. One claim fails in detail and its fix needs Amish: counted as currents rather than kilowatts, the morning block of loads draws about 55 A on paper from a 230 V, 40 A single-phase supply (S14). Two detail failures were fixed in this session under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23: motor energy is now counted at the electrical input, which moves the line from 0.83 to 0.88 kWh per kilogram of output (S13), and the feed slot is lowered from 160 to 120 mm so the ISO 13857 reach distance applies to it (S17). Five claims stay plausible rather than proven after the independent review: the press standing loss (S3), noise (S15), the feed reach until the machine-specific EN 12012 standard is checked (S17), manual lifting under real conditions (S20) and air quality (S21); most need measurements on a running site, which is TRL 4 work and on hold. Option B, the sort-and-bale hub, sits within known science; its bale density is an assumption to confirm (B1). Method and rules: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which imports the calculation note's script `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py` (RFE-CAL-001) so every figure reruns with the design.

## Fields not relevant

All five fields have rows. Within them, nuclear and ionising radiation, optics, ecology and microbiology are not relevant: nothing radioactive or optical is processed, nothing is released to land or water (wash water and sludge go to a licensed route), and the wash water's microbial load is handled by gloves and the refusal of medical waste rather than by a design figure.

## Concept options

- **Option A, reference micro-factory (the existing design, proposed to stay chosen):** S1 to S27. Open on S14 (supply current, to Amish) and on the plausible rows S3, S15, S17, S20 and S21.
- **Option B, sort-and-bale hub:** B1 to B5. Within known science; it needs about 24 A on the same single-phase supply because nothing is heated and only one motor runs. The bale mass rests on an assumed bale density until a trial bale is weighed (B1).

## Independent review

On 2026-10-25 a second agent, given only this document, the calculation note RFE-CAL-001, the product requirements RFE-PRS-001 and `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py`, and not the author's reasoning, tried to refute the headline results and every "Within known science" verdict. It recomputed the mass balance, energy, press heating, fan, hood, noise, current and reach figures and could not refute them; it confirmed S14 fails. Its findings and what changed:

| Row | Finding | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S3 | The 0.8 kW standing loss is an assumption checked against an estimate, for a closed press only | Verdict to Plausible; closed by measurement at TRL 4 |
| S4 | The check used average power over the cycle, not the heating phase | Restated: at full power the sheet's heat goes in within 36.6 min of the 55 min cycle (cooling is in the cooling press); still within |
| S10 | NFPA 92 is a fire-plume equation, used far below its range; the hot mould is a bigger short source | Cited as a heuristic; mould unloading noted |
| S11 | ACGIH does not give a 10 m/s minimum for fumes | Restated as a design choice (ACGIH about 5 to 10 m/s); wax condensation in the duct added to the design FMEA (F5a) |
| S14 | 55 A adds table currents arithmetically (conservative); block B is 91 % of the supply; option (b) costs about 13 USD a shift in wages and turns the result to about -12.7 USD; the MSEDCL source is from 2010 | All added to RFE-CAL-001 section 5 and RFE-DEC-001 D1; the fail stands |
| S16 | The open-area limit counts only the feed slot | Noted; S15 stays plausible |
| S17 | A machine-specific type C standard (EN 12012 series) takes precedence over ISO 13857; the cutter height is assumed | Verdict to Plausible until EN 12012 is checked; added to RFE-REG-001 (G8a) |
| S18 | Bending only; the service deflection is about 41 mm (L/290) and racking stiffness is unchecked | Both stated for the structural engineer |
| S20 | 25 kg is the ISO 11228-1 reference mass under ideal conditions, not a limit for repeated lifts at height | Verdict to Plausible |

| S2 | Float-sink does not split HDPE from PP | Stated: they are sorted into their own bins at the table |
| B1 | The bale density was chosen to match the listing, so the check is circular | Stated; stays plausible |
