# Recipe: PP sheets and beams

**Output:** as for HDPE (1000 x 1000 x 12 mm sheets of about 10.9 kg at about 905 kg/m³, and beams), in separate PP lots. PP and HDPE do not bond well to each other, so mixed sheets are weak; keep them apart.

## Input grade

- Rigid PP packaging (resin code 5): tubs, food containers, caps, crates. Caps skimmed from the PET float-sink step go here or to HDPE after identification.
- Rejected: woven PP sacks and film (residue unless a separate buyer exists), chemical containers, anything that might be PS or PVC.

## Steps

Steps 1 to 5 and 8 to 9 are the same as in [hdpe.md](hdpe.md). The hot steps differ:

6. **Sheet press:** press at about 190 to 210 °C for about 55 min for 12 mm; cool under load in the cooling press. PP shrinks more than HDPE on cooling, so keep it clamped until below about 60 °C.
7. **Extruder:** about 190 °C at the feed zone and 200 °C at the barrel and nozzle, as published for the Precious Plastic Extrusion Pro.

## Temperature window

The general processing range for PP is about 200 to 240 °C (melting point 160 to 170 °C). The reference line uses 190 to 210 °C and never exceeds 240 °C.

## Yield (RFE-CAL-001, estimate)

Included in the combined HDPE and PP figure: about 32.3 kg of products from 38 kg of HDPE and PP in 100 kg of input.

## Rejects

As for HDPE.

## PPE

As for HDPE.

> **Safety:** As for HDPE. PP runs about 10 °C hotter than HDPE in this recipe, which raises fume generation; check that the hood sashes are down and the fan is running before heating.
