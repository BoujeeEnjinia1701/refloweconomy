# Recipe: HDPE sheets and beams

**Output:** 1000 x 1000 mm HDPE sheets 12 mm thick (about 11.4 kg each) from the sheet press, and beams and profiles from the extruder. Sold locally for furniture, signage, planters and building uses, with a passport.

## Input grade

- Rigid HDPE packaging (resin code 2): milk, detergent and shampoo bottles, crates, caps. Natural (uncolored) in its own lot where a buyer pays for it.
- Rejected: film and bags (residue), containers that held chemicals, fuel or oil, and anything that might be PVC or PS.

## Steps

1. **Sort** by resin and color; check doubtful items with WasteWise Scan or WasteWise-ml.
2. **Pre-wash**, strip labels, cut open containers that still hold residue.
3. **Shred** to 5 to 10 mm flake, one polymer at a time.
4. **Float-sink**: HDPE floats; skim it off and discard what sinks.
5. **Dry** on trays (surface moisture only; HDPE absorbs very little water). Dried flake from one shift feeds the hot zone on the next shift.
6. **Sheet press:** preheat the press (about 1.6 h from cold on the 5 kW single-phase heaters, RFE-CAL-001). Fill the mould with 11.4 kg of flake for a 12 mm sheet, press at 190 to 200 °C for about 55 min, then move the mould to the cooling press under load until the sheet is below about 60 °C so it stays flat.
7. **Extruder:** barrel at about 190 to 200 °C for HDPE; extrude into steel beam moulds; cool the mould before release. About 5 kg/h (assumption, RFE-CAL-001).
8. **Trim** edges; re-shred trim and off-cuts into the same HDPE lot (in-house regrind is not counted as pre-consumer content in the passport).
9. **Label, weigh and passport** each sheet or bundle of beams.

## Temperature window

The general processing range for HDPE is about 190 to 230 °C. The reference line stays at the low end (190 to 200 °C at the platens and barrel) because low temperatures produce fewer fumes. Never exceed 230 °C; smoke or a sharp smell means the setting is too high.

## Yield (RFE-CAL-001, estimate)

Of the dried HDPE and PP flake, about 3 % is lost as purge and degraded material; trim of about 8 % of each sheet is re-shredded and returned. Across both polymers, 38 kg of HDPE and PP in 100 kg of input give about 32.3 kg of saleable products.

## Rejects

Burnt or discolored purge (licensed disposal); sheets with voids go back to the shredder once only, then to disposal if they fail again.

## PPE

Heat-resistant gloves and face shield at the press and extruder; long sleeves; safety boots; hearing protection near the shredder. Respirators with combined particulate and organic vapor filters are a backup, not a replacement for the hoods.

> **Safety:** Platens and barrels at 190 to 200 °C cause severe burns; the press closes with high force. Keep hands out of the press while it closes; cool down before maintenance; never leave heaters on unattended. Fumes from polyolefins include hydrocarbons, formaldehyde, acetaldehyde, formic acid and wax-like aerosol, so the hoods must run whenever a heater is on.
