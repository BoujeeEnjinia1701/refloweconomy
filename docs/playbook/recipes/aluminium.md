# Recipe: aluminium remelting and sand casting (add-on bay)

**Status:** this recipe is for an **add-on aluminium bay** that is not part of the reference micro-factory floor. Amish decided on 2026-09-25 that the reference line is plastics only and that aluminium is a separate bay with its own safety case (RFE-DDR-001, item 1). That safety case, the furnace and the bay layout are not yet designed; until they are, the reference micro-factory sells aluminium as sorted scrap.

**Output:** simple sand castings (for example weights, brackets, cookware blanks) or clean ingots for a local foundry, with a passport. Remelting aluminium takes about 5 % of the energy of primary production, which is why aluminium rates high in the feasibility matrix.

## Input grade

- Clean, dry, known aluminium: beverage cans (crushed, not sealed), clean castings, extrusions such as window frames with rubber and glass removed.
- Rejected: sealed or pressurized containers (aerosol cans), anything wet, oily or painted heavily, foil laminated to plastic, mixed metals, and anything that might contain magnesium, lithium or other reactive metals.

## Steps

1. **Sort** by alloy family (cans; castings; extrusions) and remove steel with a magnet.
2. **Dry** all scrap, tools and moulds completely; preheat scrap next to the furnace before charging. Water trapped under molten aluminium turns to steam and can cause an explosion.
3. **Charge** a preheated crucible in small amounts; never add wet or cold scrap to a melt.
4. **Melt** and bring to a pouring temperature of about 704 to 760 °C (1300 to 1400 °F).
5. **Skim** dross with a preheated, dry skimmer into a dry steel container.
6. **Pour** into dry sand moulds on a dry sand floor.
7. **Cool** and shake out; cut off gates and risers and remelt them.

## Temperature window

Pour at about 704 to 760 °C; do not overheat, which increases dross and fumes. Aluminium melts at about 660 °C.

## Yield

Not calculated in RFE-CAL-001 because the bay is not part of the reference floor. Coated cans lose part of their mass as coating and dross; the yield must be measured at the first site.

## Rejects

Dross (licensed disposal or sale to a dross processor); cans with coatings burn off smoke and must be melted under extraction.

## PPE

Aluminized or flame-resistant jacket and apron, face shield over safety glasses, foundry gloves, leather spats and safety boots, no synthetic clothing.

> **Safety:** Molten aluminium at about 700 °C causes severe burns, and molten metal in contact with water can explode. Everything that touches the melt must be dry and preheated. The add-on bay needs its own safety case, a qualified review and a dry sand floor before any melting (RFE-DDR-001, item 1).
